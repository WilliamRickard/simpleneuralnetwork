V11 experiments

Retained
1. Adaptive AVX-512 polynomial sigmoid.
   - degree 4 for |x| <= 1
   - degree 8 fallback to |x| <= 12
   - saturate outside |x| > 12
   - common benchmark activations remain inside |x| < 1
   - materially faster across every retained v11 workload

Rejected
1. Row-oriented structure-of-arrays blocked kernel, B=64.
   The 11 x 16 network is too skinny for this layout. Weight broadcasts and horizontal reductions cost more than the reduced gradient-store traffic. It was materially slower than the v10-style hidden-node SIMD kernel.

2. Row-oriented structure-of-arrays blocked kernel, B=128.
   Same conclusion. With libmvec it was roughly 2x-3x slower in screening. Replacing libmvec with the polynomial sigmoid recovered substantial time but remained slower than the v10-style kernel.

3. General degree-7 piecewise sigmoid evaluated across the full range every time.
   Faster than libmvec, but it evaluates both polynomial pieces even when all lanes are close to zero. The adaptive common-path design was significantly faster.

4. General degree-9 piecewise sigmoid evaluated across the full range every time.
   More accurate than degree 7, but slower than the adaptive design. Adaptive degree 4 + degree 8 fallback gives lower common-path cost while keeping global absolute error below 6.57e-6.

Not included in v11
- oneMKL and SLEEF were not installed in the benchmark environment, so no unverified third-party dependency was added.
- FP32/mixed precision, fast-math compiler modes, mini-batches and alternative optimisers remain candidates for later versions because they relax the numerical/training contract further than v11.
